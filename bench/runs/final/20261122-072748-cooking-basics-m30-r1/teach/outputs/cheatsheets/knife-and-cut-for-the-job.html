Choosing the knife and the cut

Look-up page for picking a knife and a cut for a job. Pick the job first, the knife second, the cut third.

The three knives

Chef's (blade 8 to 10 in)Big, firm foodsTiny peeling workOnion, carrot, chicken

The steps

  1. Ask what the job is: what is the food, and how do you want it at the end?
  2. Pick the knife from the table, because a good match needs less force.
  3. Pick the cut and its size, because even pieces cook evenly.
  4. Make the food stable (cut one flat side if it rolls), then use the claw grip.

Worked example: a potato for roast potatoes

  1. Job: pieces that finish cooking together. Knife: chef's, because the potato is firm.
  2. Cut: medium dice, 1/2 in cubes.
  3. Cut 1/2 in slabs, stack them, cut 1/2 in sticks, turn the sticks and cut across every 1/2 in.
  4. Result: 1/2 in cubes, all the same size.

Common mistakes

  • "The small knife is the safe one." A paring knife on a hard carrot forces you to push.
  • "A serrated knife is only for bread." It also suits tomatoes and oranges.
  • "Dice means chop it small." Dice is a cube with a size, and mixed sizes cook unevenly.
  • "Any thin strip is julienne." Julienne is 1/8 in square.
  • Cutting a round carrot as it is. It rolls, so flatten one side first.
